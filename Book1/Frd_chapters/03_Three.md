# Three

I found out later what he did while I was asleep. I'm not exactly sure when it stopped being *it* in my head, I only noticed I'd already done it - the way you notice, well after the fact, that you've started calling someone by their first name.

When I came around the corner of the mill with my coat still half-buttoned and my eyes not fully decided about being open, he was there. Something about the way he was standing — not the stillness, which was always there, but the quality of it — made me stop walking about a dozen paces out.

I stood there for a moment. He stood there for a moment.

His eye found me — the glow settling, one lid dropping fractionally as it fixed on me.

"Did you sleep?" I asked, because I was not yet awake enough for a more sophisticated opening.

There was a bellows-wheeze, slow and considered, from somewhere inside his chest. It vented the smell of something old, before the avalanche spoke. "I do not need sleep. However, during the dark I did advise someone that attempting a non-front-door entry during non-open hours would not be in their best interest. They then left."

I thought about that for a moment. My heart was doing what it had been doing since I'd turned the corner and seen him — the specific rhythm of a body that hasn't yet caught up to the decision the mind already made.

"If that's the truth," I said, "I think we can offer you something on the house. Or whatever you need for—" I looked at his face, which had no mouth. "—nourishment."

"I require only to be indoors where I do not need to consider night attacks from directions I am not watching." The eye didn't move from me. "Tondi was to arrange this. It appears I have circumvented that requirement by standing watch outside your inn."

"You are," I said, "genuinely resourceful."

I walked the remaining dozen paces and stopped at the door. He came aside in the way he had — the whole body moving as a single considered unit, not the unconscious shuffle of someone making room. He chose to move. That was the thing about Third, I was already beginning to understand. Everything was chosen.

At the lock I asked what else he'd done with the night. He told me in pieces.

He'd spent most of it considering the memorial pillar. Not looking at it — he'd read it before I'd arrived, all the names, the founding plaque worn smooth at the edges. He was considering it from memory, which for Third was not a figure of speech. He had the names of everyone who had ever died in this town stored somewhere in whatever served him for a mind, and had been cross-referencing them against what he knew of the region, the trail, the kinds of things that tended to happen to small settlements within a certain range of certain other things.

I asked him what he'd concluded. He said the town was not in immediate danger. That the kind of threat he was currently occupied with tended toward specific targets rather than casual ones, and this town was not specific enough to be a target yet. That yet was doing a lot of work in that sentence, and he knew it, and I knew he knew it.

I asked him why he hadn't said any of this the night before. He said I hadn't asked. I said that was technically true and also a very annoying answer. He said he knew that too.

He'd stayed at the window the rest of the night, he said — for no reason he could name precisely. The night was the same. The patrol was the same. The town was the same. It had simply seemed the right place to be when morning came, if morning was going to bring what he thought it might.

Once inside, i put away my coat and got him to a seat. "What did you think morning was going to bring?"

He said: someone willing to ask how soon.

"Looks like it might get stony today," I said — the sky had that particular flat quality that usually meant falling rocks by mid-morning.

I started the morning routine — shutters, stove, stew selection — and did not think about the fact that there was a Titanspawn Forged sitting in the corner of my inn wearing its worn cloak, and watching me with one blue eye.

I was not thinking about it very hard.
