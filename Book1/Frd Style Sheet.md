# *FRD* — Style Sheet

### A novel of Anka · Christopher Aruwa

A reference for any proofreader, copyeditor, or grammar tool working on this manuscript. Its purpose is to mark the difference between an **error** and an **intentional choice**, so that the second kind survives editing. *Updated October 2026 to match the finished chapters, which are now canon.*

> **The golden rule of this manuscript:** *When something looks “wrong,” check this sheet before changing it.* A great deal of what reads as a mistake here is deliberate — the fragments, the lowercase world-words, the British spelling, the missing reverence-charm in one character’s mouth. Fixing those quietly damages the book. Genuine typos and inconsistencies, of course, should be corrected; a short list of known ones is at the end.

-----

## 0. Title

The book is titled **FRD** — the Titanspeech abjad rendering of *Third* (TH → F, vowels unwritten). Title page, copyright page, metadata and cover all read **FRD**, with the subtitle *a novel of Anka*. **“Third” is the character’s name** and stays “Third” in the text and in every reference document; only the book’s title is FRD.

-----

## 1. Dialect & spelling — **British English throughout**

The manuscript uses British spelling and should stay consistent in it. Do **not** Americanise.

|Use (BrE)                              |Not (AmE)                         |
|---------------------------------------|----------------------------------|

|centre, metre                          |center, meter                     |
|realise, recognise, apologise, organise|realize, recognize, …             |

“**grey**” appears ~150 times and is also a load-bearing world-term (see §4). Never change it to “gray.”

## 2. Voice & intentional style — **do not normalise**

These are deliberate and pervasive. A grammar checker will flag every one of them. Leave them.

- **Sentence fragments for rhythm.** Used constantly, for weight and breath:
  *“He was still alive, as far as anyone knew. He was just elsewhere.”* · *“Not toward the carts. Past them, somehow, at an angle I couldn’t have drawn.”* · *“He wasn’t there.”*
  Do not join these to neighbouring sentences or “complete” them.
- **Sentences opening with And / But / Because / So.** Intentional. Do not rewrite.
- **Single-word or single-line paragraphs.** Used for emphasis (*“I left it there.”*). Keep them on their own line.
- **The intrusive voice — italic, present tense, unattributed.** Third’s old compulsion speaks in italics with no dialogue tag: *“You are Forged to eliminate the Virek.”* These are not speech and not Third’s own thoughts; never add quotation marks, never add “he thought,” never change the tense.
- **Interior thought in italics**, likewise without quotation marks (*Her.* · *Bada.* · *May they never be forgotten.*). Leave unpunctuated by quotes.
- **The grey’s voice (ch30)** is lowercase italics with no capitals — deliberate.
- **Comma splices and long breath-sentences** appear by design in Tasha’s narration, especially at high emotion. Don’t break them into tidy short sentences; the run *is* the feeling.
- **“the way you…” / “the kind of…” constructions** (Tasha’s signature). Repetition is voice, not redundancy.
- **Repeated tics as characterisation:** *“I’m good at not thinking about things”*; *“I want that on the record”*; *“You only get to … for free the first time.”* Intentional callbacks — do not vary for elegance.
- **Child and dialect speech** is spelled as heard (Wally in ch13: *“wif,” “sumfin,” “leefs,” “breaving”*; Mathen’s *“m’self”*). Leave.
- **Sougad’s crossing word is never written out.** The text says *“an old word”* / *“the other old word”* (ch19, ch36, ch38). Do not supply the word.

### 2a. The reverence-tic — **the most important “do not fix”**

In Anka, naming the lost first people, the **Virek**, carries a reflexive verbal charm: **“may they never be forgotten.”**

- **Ordinary speakers append it** without thinking (Kern, the militia, Tondi, Mathen). Keep it.
- **Third never appends it** — because he actually remembers them and won’t perform the ritual. This omission is deliberate and load-bearing.
- **Tasha notices the difference** (ch. 5) and, once, swallows her own reflex.

A copyeditor seeing the charm in one mouth and absent in another will read an inconsistency and “standardise” it. **Do not.** The inconsistency is the point. Never add the charm to Third’s lines; never strip it from others’.

## 3. Time, distance & measurement

Anka is a flat plane with no sun, moon, stars, or seasons; the **Scar** (a rotating rift) is the only clock. The prose reflects this.

- **Elapsed time is counted in *spins*** (one rotation of the Scar). e.g. *“about 1,200 spins,” “three hundred thousand spins.”* Never convert to years.
- **A *tenday*** is the week-unit. Keep as one word, lowercase. (*“half a tenday”* is fine.)
- **No seasons, years, months, or weeks** anywhere (also not *centuries, decades*). If one appears, it’s an error: recast in spins or tendays.
- **Within-cycle time** uses the Scar and daylight words: *dawn, midday, afternoon, evening, dark, night, first light, the Scar’s crossing.*
- **Hours, minutes and seconds are accepted in-world** (the world is clockwork-literate; the Scar’s fuzzy edge takes about five minutes to cross). Use them as ordinary idiom and measurement. Do not use *o’clock* or numbered clock-times.
- **No sun, moon or stars — and don’t name a source of warmth.** Ambient light and warmth are simply *the day’s* (“the day’s warmth,” “the open sky”). The Scar is the *dark*; the Spire is a landmark, not a lamp.
- **Distance is in *paces*, *strides*, *a stone’s throw* (open, no hyphen), *hand-widths*, body-heights** (“six men high”) and arch-heights (“ten of the Causeway’s arches”). **Never feet, yards, miles, metres or kilometres.**
- **Direction is by the Spire:** *spireward / rimward / spinward / anti-spinward.* Lowercase except at sentence start. Keep the hyphen in *anti-spinward.* (*“anti-spin”* appears once or twice in a Hin’s dialect — leave.)

**Lowercase by design** (do **not** capitalise these — they are common nouns in this world):

- **the grey** — Akna as people live beside it. Lowercase, with article: *“the grey,” “into the grey.”*
- **magefire** — the blue-white caged lightning. Always lowercase, one word.
- **hexwood** — Titan-blood-hardened wood. Lowercase, one word (capital only at a sentence start).
- **glaive** — the Titan-made pale polearm Third leaves at the brook on his first day (ch16, ch40). Lowercase.
- **spins, tenday, spireward, rimward, spinward, anti-spinward, stony** (the rock-weather) — all lowercase.

**“The cold man”** is reserved for Sougad (from ch33 on). Never use it for Third.

## 5. Punctuation & formatting

- **Em dash —** with surrounding spaces ( — ), used heavily for interruption and aside. Keep the spacing consistent. Do not convert to en dashes, and do not use a spaced hyphen ( - ) as a dash. Hyphens are for compounds only.
- **Ellipsis …** is the single character, for trailing-off speech. Keep sparing. Never type three full stops.
- **Quotation marks:** straight double quotes throughout the chapter files; thought and the intrusive voice use italics with **no** quotes (§2). Single quotes only for quotes-within-quotes and the occasional scare-quote (*‘icing on the cake’*).
- **Scene break within a chapter:** centred `* * *`, written in the chapter files as `<p align="center">&#42; &#42; &#42;</p>`. Marks a time/POV shift inside a scene-cluster.
- **Memory break:** centred `◆ ◆ ◆`, written as `<p align="center">&#9670; &#9670; &#9670;</p>`. Marks the boundary between Tasha’s present-tense narration and Third’s “grey” memory passages. **These two dividers are not interchangeable** — never swap one for the other, and don’t delete them (they carry the braided structure). Keep them as HTML entities; bare asterisks get mangled in round-trips.
- **No chapter titles.** Chapters are numbered only (One … Forty One). The numbered scheme de-spoilers the table of contents; don’t reintroduce descriptive titles.
- **Dialogue** uses double quotes.

-----

## 6. Numbers

- **Spin-counts and large figures: numerals with thousands separators** — *1,200 spins; 4,800 spins; 5,100 spins; three hundred thousand spins* (this last is spelled out for cadence — both styles appear by intent; don’t force one).
- Small incidental counts may be spelled out (*“two paces,” “six tables”*). Don’t impose a rigid numeral threshold; match the surrounding cadence.

## 8. Known items to correct (genuine, not style)

1. The boy’s line at the brook, *“A Blank just used I,”* appears in three punctuations (ch16 L95 `used I?`, ch22 L23 `used I.`, ch40 L45 `used 'I'?`). Pick one and use it in all three.
2. Otherwise, treat the manuscript as having passed a continuity + copyedit pass (Oct 2026). Flag anything new you find, but **default to querying rather than silently changing** anything covered in §§2–6.

-----

### How to use this sheet with a grammar tool

Most automated checkers (Grammarly, ProWritingAid, Word’s editor) will object to §2 (fragments, sentence-initial conjunctions, comma splices) and may try to “correct” §4 lowercase terms. Run them for **spelling and genuine typos only**, and ignore style/grammar suggestions that touch anything documented here. When in doubt, query the author — don’t change.
