## The Scar

The defining feature of Anka’s sky, and the reason the world has anything like a “day” at all. As the story goes, when the Titan of storms was sundered, it tore a long rift across the sky on its way down — and that rift has been rotating overhead, slow and steady, ever since.

As the Scar turns, it carries its rift across the sky, plunging whatever lies beneath into darkness and then carrying it back into light again, with a regularity nothing else on Anka quite matches. The edges of the rift aren’t sharp — they’re said to be “fuzzy” — which is why the change from bright to dark (or back again) takes a few minutes rather than happening all at once; travelers get roughly five minutes’ warning before it actually arrives. Locals don’t bother with separate words for “day” and “night” the way other worlds do. There’s just “day,” with the Scar somewhere in it, dividing the bright part from the dark.

The Scar’s rotation is also the reason Anka has no moons and no stars: whatever’s up there, the Scar is the only thing in it.


## Titan Footprints -- texture note (locked): 
Not uniform. Some stretches read as dragged and swept (furrows, knuckled stone, smooth walls); others — as established in Book 1, ch10 — read as violently broken: shattered, fractured, “a mouth full of broken teeth.” Whatever moved through left different marks in different places, possibly depending on what it was dragging, how it was moving, or how long ago the ground gave way. Do not standardize to one texture.

## Chipwood (locked)

A small settlement two days spinward of Dunarth — "the next notch down the line" from Tasha's town: smaller, further out, with a memorial pillar, a militia, a mill and a lockbox. Home of the traveller who staggers into Tondi's inn in Book 1 (ch6) with the news that it is gone. It is destroyed off-page, by the Ravager surge, before he reaches Tasha's town. It is **not** the settlement the party searches in ch17 (a separate place with its own memorial pillar). Its fate beyond that is open; flagged for possible foreshadowing in a future book.
