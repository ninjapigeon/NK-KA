# Titanspeech — Working Dictionary
*A reference for drafting, extending the Third World Bible v15 Titanspeech glossary (Part One / Part Eleven).*

## 1. What's locked vs. what's new
The 16 roots in the World Bible are canon and unchanged here. Everything else below is new working
vocabulary, built to match their pattern, so you can pull from it while drafting without inventing on
the fly. Treat it as a *draft appendix* to canon.md, not as locked text — promote entries to canon.md
as they get used in prose.

## 2. Orthography & phonology
- Written Titanspeech is a true **abjad**: only consonants are written. A reader/speaker supplies
  whatever vowels the context calls for — this is in-world canon (Third ACT THREE: "carries no vowels
  of its own and borrows whatever ones you lend it").
- Consonant inventory used here: **B D F G H J K L M N P R S T V W Z**.
- Roots are 2–4 consonants. Two-consonant roots tend to be old/primal concepts (NK, KR, KT); three- and
  four-consonant roots are more specific or derived (VRK, HMN, KF).

## 3. The grammar of inversion (locked device)
Reversing a root's consonant order does not always mean "opposite" in a tidy dictionary sense — it means
**thematically bound-but-opposed**: NK/KN (solid world / un-making world), KT/TK (take / lose ownership),
LKR/RKL (rich / poor), VRK/KRV (the Virek / the genocide they committed), HMN/NMH, KF/HTK. Use this
device sparingly and only where the opposition is *meaningful*, not mechanically on every root — it's a
rhetorical device in-world (a scholar's or a bargainer's trick, per Sougad's own speech patterns), not a
regular grammatical rule.

### 2a. The letter F (replaces the TH digraph) — Keth renamed Kef
Titanspeech has **no TH digraph**. The letter **F** covers that whole articulation range. This is now a
full rename, not just a spelling note: the people are **Kef**, not Keth, in prose and in Titanspeech
alike. Written root: **KF**.
- Practical consequence: the abstract-noun suffix is now **-F**, not -TH (KTF "ownership", BKRF
  "authority"). Only one generated root was affected otherwise (VDTH → **VDF**, "peace").
- This has been applied in canon.md (locked glossary + the two "Keth-only" / "Keth-invented" lines).
- **Manuscript find-replace still needed** — this dictionary and canon.md can't reach into the actual
  Book 1/Book 2 files. Every prose instance of "Keth" needs to become "Kef" (and "Kethish"/"Kethian"
  forms, if any exist, to "Kefish"/"Kefian"). Worth a dedicated pass rather than a blind find-replace,
  since "Keth" won't collide with other words but you'll want to check capitalization and any
  possessive forms by hand.

### 2b. BKR — Titan *and* power (one word)
**BKR is both "Titan" and "power."** Not two senses of a word — one word, and Titanspeech never
distinguished them. Which meaning came first is unrecoverable in-world, and that is the point: the
language cannot say "power" without saying "Titan," and cannot say "Titan" without asserting that it
*is* power. The narcissism is baked into the grammar rather than stated anywhere.

Three things fall out of this that are worth keeping:
- **The agent form is defective.** BKRR ("one who holds power") is grammatically well-formed and
  never used. There is no word for a power-holder, because the language admits only one, and it
  already has a name. Sorcerers, emperors and the Virek are all described with borrowed or
  circumlocuted terms instead. A scholar pointing this out is a good half-page of Book 2 lore.
- **The B- augmentative now has an etymology.** The Titan-scale intensifier prefix (BKT "to seize
  utterly") is a worn-down BKR. Every intensified word in the language is literally saying
  "Titan-ly." Speakers stopped hearing it centuries ago, which is the most convincing kind of
  fossil.
- **The reversal gets sharper.** Locked canon already has RKB = "the races who rose against them."
  With BKR = power, RKB simultaneously reads as *the powerless* — and the word for the uprising is
  the word for power said backwards. Sougad in particular would enjoy that.

One note of caution: this merge makes BKR extremely load-bearing. If you want ordinary, unremarkable
"strength" or "force" in mundane dialogue, use a Qualities-table root (see §5) rather than BKR — BKR
should always land with weight.

## 4. Productive derivation (how 435 roots become 1000+ usable words)
Titanspeech doesn't mark part-of-speech the way English does — word order and context do that, same as
the unwritten vowels. But it does have a small, productive affix set. Applied to the root list below,
these comfortably take the working vocabulary past 1000 addressable word-forms without needing 1000
separately-invented roots (this mirrors how real root-and-pattern languages work — a few hundred roots,
multiplied by derivation, cover thousands of words):

| Affix | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *(bare root)* | concept, verb, or noun by context | **KT** — take / a taking |
| **-R** (suffix) | agent noun, "one who [root]s" | **KTR** — a taker, a thief |
| **-F** (suffix) | abstract noun, "the state/quality of [root]" | **KTF** — possession, ownership |
| **H-** (prefix) | negation, "not-/un-[root]" | **HKT** — to refuse, to not-take |
| **-M** (suffix) | plural / collective | **KTM** — takings, a haul |
| **-K** (suffix) | diminutive / lesser instance | **KTK** — a small theft, a pilfering |
| **B-** (prefix) | augmentative, Titan-scale intensifier (a worn-down BKR) | **BKT** — to seize utterly, to unmake by taking |
| reversal | inversion (see §3) — use selectively | **TK** — to lose ownership |

Applying even three of these affixes across the 435 roots below yields well over 1,300 distinct
word-forms; you will not use most of them, but the *system* is what needs to be consistent, not a
memorized 1000-line list. Below is a worked sample so the pattern is visible before the bulk glossary.

### Worked example (root: BKR, "Titan / power")
| Form | Gloss |
|---|---|
| BKR | power (concept/verb: "to hold power") — and *Titan*; one word, see §2b |
| BKRR | *defective* — no agent form in use; see §2b |
| BKRF | authority, the state of holding power |
| HBKR | powerlessness, to be without power; to be un-Titaned |
| BKRM | powers (plural) — grammatically possible, in-world nonsense |
| BKRK | a small or borrowed power, a trick |
| BBKR | overwhelming power (the B- prefix doubling on its own source) |
| RKB | (reversal) — locked canon: the races who rose against them; now also *the powerless* |

## 6. Using this in prose
- Keep the same register split as canon.md: formal/scholarly Titanspeech words belong in epigraphs,
  Sougad's speech, and Third's grey-memory register — not in Tasha's or Adura's casual narration.
- New roots you actually use in prose should get copied into canon.md as they're coined, the same way
  the 16 locked roots got there, so this file stays a scratch appendix and canon.md stays the single
  source of truth.
- The reversal device (§3) is best saved for moments that want the weight of it — Sougad naming things,
  a scholar's aside, an epigraph — not routine dialogue.
