## THE TITAN — COSMOLOGY

**One being. One war. One pattern.**

The Virek's war was not fought against multiple Titans. It was
fought against one being — an elemental child — whose fixations
manifested as distinct aspects: the glutton, the hunter
(Herduk), the forge-master (under the mountain), the magic-aspect
(Methos, Virek-assigned name), the sky, the venom, the shaper, and
others unnamed. Moods categorised as separate beings by the Virek
— not wrong, not complete. The multiple-Titan narrative most
people now believe is oral drift from this original
misunderstanding.

The Virek, studying their enemy in secret, concluded the aspects
were independent beings — reasoning that "one cannot assume a
hurricane is interested in what an earthquake is doing." They
planned accordingly: take each aspect down separately, exploit the
fact that none would defend the others.

They were right for the wrong reason. The Titan *couldn't*
coordinate its own aspects in its own defense — not because they
were separate, but because a child's mind is entirely in whatever
it is currently fixated on. The glutton-aspect did not notice what
was happening to the magic-aspect. The hunter-aspect did not come
to the forge-master's aid. The Virek assumed no coordination
because they believed they were fighting separate entities. They
got no coordination because they were fighting one entity with a
child's attention span. The strategy that saved Anka was built on
a false premise that happened to be tactically correct. The Virek
never knew.

**The cycle of aspects.**

When an aspect was defeated — disconnected from Anka, stripped of
its anchoring tools — it did not die. It fell into Akna,
reconstituted, and returned in a different form. The glutton-
aspect is the clearest example: Virek sorcerers pulled its teeth
one by one. Each tooth was simultaneously an anchor to Anka and a
processing mechanism — without them the glutton could not
metabolize what it consumed, could not draw power from Anka, could
not stay connected to the physical world. Disconnected and
starving, it fell into Akna. It came back as another aspect. The
war continued.

This cycle repeated across aspects and generations until
Titansfall — when the Virek finally hit it hard enough, or right
enough, that it fragmented rather than reconstituted cleanly. The
result is the Titan as it exists today: not dead, fragmented,
partially in Akna, its movement felt as earthquakes, its essence
touching the battlefield at Titansfall and waking the Forged.

Why the Virek ultimately won: coordination. The Titan's aspects
never defended each other. The Virek exploited one vulnerability
of pure elemental selfishness — across many separate campaigns,
not realizing until much later (if ever) that it was the same
selfishness, the same child, every time.

**The Gnawing Fields, recontextualized.**

The white plinths of the Gnawing Fields are not a dead Titan's
scattered remains in the abstract — they are the glutton-aspect's
pulled teeth specifically, belonging to the same being that is now
fragmented under the mountain, the same being whose essence woke
Third at Titansfall. The teeth stayed behind because they were
anchors, not organs. They are still technically part of the Titan.
Still warm to the touch. Still faintly doing what teeth do.

Sougad pulls pins. The Virek pulled teeth. Two strategies against
the same underlying problem — disconnecting the Titan from Anka —
separated by ~300,000 spins. The Virek didn't know they were
establishing a template. Sougad may or may not know he's following
one.

**Titan aspect tools — shared maker.**

All Titanic artifacts are made by the same being in different
periods of fixation. The glutton-aspect's tools are consumption
and anchoring instruments. The forge-aspect's tools are
transformation instruments. The hunter-aspect's tools are pursuit
and reading instruments. The magic-aspect's tools are the Forged
themselves and the magefire that powers them. They share a maker's
signature — Titan-willed material (see Materials & Economy) — but
different aspects carry different registers of will.

Third can recognize Titan-willed material as a category because
his own body is made of it. He cannot reliably attribute an
artifact to a specific aspect — he knows his own maker's hand
because it IS his own body; other aspects feel related but wrong,
the same handwriting in a different mood.

Sougad collecting Titan-willed objects is, at the deepest level,
the Titan — through its instrument — drawing its own scattered
pieces back toward itself. Sougad does not know this. The Titan
does not know this. It is simply what happens when a made thing
tries to return to its maker.

## THE ANVIL

The Titan-under-the-mountain's primary forging tool. Base was
once a mountain, ground down across the making of the Forged. The
Glaive was made on this surface. Lost into Akna at Titansfall.
Sougad grabbed it — the only solid object in a plane with no solid
ground. His soul-stone by accident. Not his by right.

The Graveyard's cause: the cliff is the mountain's root, embedded
half in Anka, half in Akna. Anvil resonance causes all anomalous
behaviour there (Boltcasters unreliable, Third's side-step turbulent).

**Why Sougad needs Third (door vs. passenger; the consent
mechanism) — locked:**

The Anvil is Sougad's anchor. His soul is pinned to it; he is a
nail in the wall of the world, not a window. The Anvil sits in
Akna and is too vast to move — so everything currently "sucks"
toward the cliff: the Anvil pulling the grey, and all the warm
dead in it, homeward. The cliff "drinking the world" IS the Anvil
pulling.

Sougad can step sideways into the grey — he is its passenger — so
he can REACH the Anvil, but reaching is not OPENING. Stepping
sideways moves one body across the membrane; the ritual needs the
membrane itself held wide and long enough for the thing under
everything to come through into Anka. An anchored thing can cross;
it cannot become an aperture. He can stand at the anchor and pull;
pulling is not opening.

Third IS the door because of what he's made of: forged on/through
the Anvil (his making rang through it — which is how Sougad felt
the Forged made), then woken by the Titan's own essence at
Titansfall ("my essence has awoken you"). He does not cross the
boundary; he IS a seam in it. The same essence that woke him is
the thing that wants through. Third is the only existing place
where Anka and Akna are the same object, and that object has a
will — a will that can consent.

A wall is opened from within, by the part of it that is alive.
Third is "the only living part of the wall." Hence: the ritual
needs him willing; willing cannot be forced, only bought; hence
the lure (the dead). The consent is the entire mechanism.

Failsafe rationale: a seam that is TORN does not open a lasting
door — it snaps shut, unmakes the door (wall again). So destroying
the living seam (Dorn's Boltcaster through the chest seam / the
magefire lights) unmakes the door permanently. Killing Third = no
door = the Titan stays out.

Sougad's patience explained: he has known for a very long time
that a woken Forged was the prize, and has waited/hunted for one
(he "felt them made through the Anvil"). This does NOT require him
to know Third specifically — the asymmetry holds: he waited for A
door; Third is the one who walked up.

Stone of the Titans = a SEPARATE peg/artifact (one of the pins
holding the Titan out — among the things Sougad has been
pulling/collecting; the artifact losses of Book 1). Distinct from
the Anvil, which is Sougad's own anchor and the place the door
must open.

## AKNA / THE GREY

PHYSICS: No gravity, no up or down, little solid ground. A
swirling misty void of grey, purple, and dark blue. Occasional
mysterious winds and mystical storms; otherwise it changes little.

TIME: Passes ~100x faster in Akna than in Anka. A long stretch
spent there returns a traveler to Anka after only a handful of
spins. Load-bearing: it's why Sougad's isolation is subjectively
geological, and why Third's side-step costs "seconds" in Anka
while a memory plays out long.

PERVASION (the side-step engine): Akna does not merely touch
Anka — it pervades it. A being who steps "beside" the world exists
in both at once. From inside Akna, Anka shows as hazy,
incorporeal images — smears of pale light. From Anka's side, a
being in Akna is invisible. To cross an obstacle, you move through
the grey to the spot where it "crosses over" past the obstruction,
and step back in. "Beside," never "through" — Third corrects this
distinction.

ACCESS IS SCARCE: Almost no one crosses deliberately. Most who end
up in Akna did not choose it — Sougad was BANISHED there; rifts
near villages are involuntary and random. Third is UNIQUE: the only Forged (only being) known to side-step at will. The ability unsettles people; he explains it rarely.

OTHER ROUTES IN (Book 1 ch19, ch20, ch28, ch36, ch38): sorcerers can reach Akna with the right spell, paying in blood, time or sense, usually for a single crossing; ordinary people stumble in at thin places by accident and, if lucky, come out again changed; and Sougad, banished there, crosses by saying an old word (a different word for each direction — never written out) and, unlike any other sorcerer, is not spent by it. Third alone needs no word and pays no price. "Akna" is the formal word, "the grey" the everyday one.

SOURCE: Akna is the wellspring of elemental energies and "strange
things." It is where the Titan partly retreated (fragmented, not
dead), where Sougad is stranded, where the Anvil fell.

WHERE STORIES GO TO DIE — HIN & THE GREY (locked): The grey is an
un-making place — the dissolution-side of creation, where made
things stream back and come apart. It keeps faint "almosts" of
what dissolves there: the residue of material lives. This is why
Third can "visit" his dead in the grey as echoes, and why the
Titan can weaponise that — there is enough of a person left as an
almost to ache toward.

A STORY has no material residue. A story is pure telling. Carried
into the grey, it does not leave even an almost — it dies utterly.
Hence: the grey is the place WHERE STORIES GO TO DIE. Since every
Hin IS a story that survived, a Hin in the grey would unravel
completely — not be kept, not echo, simply cease. So NO HIN CAN
EVER BE IN THE GREY. This is ontological, not legal.

RECASTS "stories do not sunder": a story is IMMUNE to the
sundering (a making/splitting ritual cannot divide pure telling)
— but MORTAL to the grey (the un-making). Two opposite cosmic
operations; the story survives one and is undone by the other.

WALLY IMPLICATIONS: Wally's fade after Liwon's death is
WORLD-SIDE — the telling that anchored him lost the one it was
told to, and "the world began to ask for him back." It is NOT a
grey-death. Wally persists by being TOLD; while the good part is
told, he is not wholly gone. But Wally can NEVER follow Third into
the grey — the one refuge Third has is the one place his
story-friend cannot go, and a Hin who stepped sideways would
simply end.

CONGEALED SOLIDITY (rare): In a plane with almost no solid ground,
the rare places where the ether congeals into solid matter are
remarkable and disorienting (no clear top or bottom). The Anvil is
the supreme instance.

EMPTY PRESENT, HAUNTED PAST (locked): The present-day grey is
mostly EMPTY — grey, silent, lonely. This emptiness is doing the
work: it is why Sougad's isolation is unbearable, why Third's
crossings feel like the inside of grief. Do NOT populate the
present grey with traffic. BUT it has a deep past: the Titan-era
touched it, and there may be derelict structures, foundered
"ships," or congealed islands left over — encountered as ruins and
rumor, never as a busy, living realm, belonging to no named race.

EPIGRAPH: The poet-philosopher Dev Nav Ral — "I have stood at the
edge of the grey, and felt every tale I have ever told grow thin
and quiet, as though it too were being read for the last time."

"THE GREY" (canonical in-prose name; AKNA = the formal name): In
narrative prose, the plane is called "the grey" — Third's own
word, adopted by Tasha. The formal name is "Akna," literally the
Titanspeech opposite of Anka (NK solid / KN nether). Third's
framing: "the grey" is the true/lived name; he learns "Akna" later
and treats the grey as truer.

REGISTER SPLIT:
  - PROSE / Third's & Tasha's voices -> "the grey" (default,
    always).
  - FORMAL / scholarly / lore / epigraph / this World Bible's own
    reference voice -> "Akna."
  - "the Nether" / "the Twisting Nether" / "githyanki": fully
    RETIRED, not just as living factions but as words. No
    exceptions. A foundered, nameless wreck glimpsed in the grey
    is fine as "haunted past" — it belongs to no named race.

# PART THREE — THIRD'S APPEARANCE

**Single eye** — a glowing HOLE in the face, lit from within,
blue. Not a lens. Not a mechanical aperture. A hole in smooth
featureless wood that happens to glow.

Expression is achieved through:
  BRIGHTNESS — the glow intensifying = attention, alertness
  DIMMING — the glow softening = grief, weight, processing
  LID POSITIONS (single, as only one hole):
    fully open = neutral / watchful
    dropped slightly = dry humour, scepticism
    half-closed = consideration, patience
    briefly closed = something like a sigh, resignation
    fully closed = torpor, or a rare moment of peace

~~~The head does not follow the eye. The eye moves to find its
subject; the head remains still. This dissociation is the primary
uncanny quality of Third's face.~~~
As there is no *eye* (only a glow in the hole in Third's head), the head must move, or the glow change in brightness, or the lids change position to deliver visible/described emotion.

PROSE GUIDANCE: RETIRED — "lens settling," "lens of it," "lens
finding," "lens rotating," any lens language whatsoever. AVAILABLE
— "the glow found me," "his eye fixed on me," "the brightness
shifted," "the lid dropped," "the eye dimmed," "the glow
settling," "the lid closed briefly." Reference: Atomic Robo
(webcomic), Robo's eye expressiveness.

**Full body description (consolidated):**

Wood-and-vine bodies as tough as Hexwood. Joints and apparent
musculature are leather-like vine fibre — but not actual leather
or vine. The Titan willed the Forged to look as though they had
muscles, so it made something that *behaves* like leather-and-vine
without being either. This is Titan-willed material (see Materials
& Economy) — the resemblance is the point; the composition is
irrelevant to the Titan and unknown to everyone else.

No runes, no veins, no hexagonal chest. Interior is Titan-willed
clockwork: gears and rods spinning in configurations that HURT THE
MIND of any who see them, because they make no sense yet keep him
alive. Not mere mechanism — Titan WILL animates the Forged; the
gears alone could not. Glimpsing his insides (an opened seam) is
its own small horror.

Single eye set off-centre on the face (full canon above). Each arm
splits at the elbow into two forearms — four forearms total when
fully extended, each ending in a three-digit hand (one thumb, two
fingers). Twelve digits in all when fully unfolded. Most Forged
keep the configuration folded in ordinary use; the split is
visible as a seam from the elbow through the palm when folded, full bifurcation when
extended. Designed for: wielding a weapon in each hand
simultaneously; wielding dual two-hand weapons; improved grappling with creatures larger than a
Forged; rapid scaling of cliff faces, fortified walls, and terrain
the Virek and Humins built deliberately inaccessible. The Titan's
army was built to take every advantage away from the people it was
fighting.

Reverse-knee legs with twin pointed, cloven, goat-like wooden
hooves. Pneumatic bellows — sparse, maximum two instances per
scene, at moments of genuine weight only. Voice like stone on
stone, "...felt through the feet or chest, before registering as heard in the ears."

Carries Falkas as weapons (the Glaive was left at the brook on his first day — see Part Two). Keeps Dorn's Boltcaster as a memento until he leaves it at the foot of the spine (Book 1, ch39). Does NOT use
spellfire. Can side-step into Akna. "Istvan" — retired name, all
instances replaced with Third.

**THE GLAIVE — APPEARANCE AND HISTORY [REVISED per Book 1]:**
The Glaive is Titan-made, not Hexwood. Titan-made weapons and

were never anointed with Titan blood. They are Titan will expressed
directly into matter, which produces its own aesthetic. The Glaive

almost skeletal, distinctly unlike anything Humin or Keth craft

It cuts through Hexwood-anointed items. Hexwood is Titan blood

**THIRD'S ARMOR — HISTORY [REVISED]:**

HEXWOOD DID NOT EXIST PRE-TITANSFALL. Geysers — where Titan blood
reaches the surface — are a post-Titansfall phenomenon. The dying
Titan's circulatory system leaks into the world; pre-Titansfall,
the blood was contained within a living being and never reached
the surface at all. No geysers → no accessible Titan blood → no
Hexwood. The Virek fought with jade, stone, and bone. The Forged
were made to fight those enemies. Hexwood is entirely a
post-Titansfall material. This also means the Dreadnoughts were
designed to hunt jade-and-stone-armed armies, not Hexwood-armed
ones — the cooking-fire attractor (see Fauna) was what the Titan
built to find organized armies, regardless of what weapons they
carried.

Pre-Titansfall, Third functionally did not need armor. His body is
Titan-willed material and the era's weapons — jade, stone, bone —
damaged him negligibly. He wore armor anyway as issued kit, the
way a Forged carried a Glaive: not because it was essential, but
because that was what the Forged were. Equipment of war, worn
because that was the role.

The armor is Titan-willed articulated plating — overlapping
plates with small interlocking teeth on each plate's edge, on
compression (impact) catching adjacent plates and locking them
together to distribute force, and on extension (movement)
releasing to preserve flexibility. A one-way ratchet principle
applied to armor. Bone-colored like all Titan-made items — pale
ivory, never Hexwood red. Articulated at every joint Third has.
In Titan-era terms, it was functional kit. By post-Titansfall
standards it is an extraordinary artifact — irreplaceable,
impossible to reproduce, worth a fortune to anyone who recognized
what it was. Third's relationship to this fact is the same as his
relationship to the Glaive's value: he has never been good at
understanding what things are worth to other people.

UNRELATED TO KETH SERPENT SCALE (locked): the interlocking-tooth
principle is not unique to Third's armor. Keth crafters arrive at
the same mechanical solution independently, roughly 10,000 spins
after Titansfall — see Materials & Economy, "Armor & Shields." The
resemblance is convergent, not derived: two makers, worlds apart,
solving the same problem (flexible protection under repeated
impact) by the same basic logic, centuries before either could
have known about the other. Third's armor is not called
"serpent-scale" and has no name in common use — it predates the
term by tens of thousands of spins and has no connection to the
Keth who eventually coined it.

THE RENUNCIATION — after Titansfall, in the period before he found
the Virek village and raised Liwon, Third left the armor. The
moment is deliberately quieter than the Glaive moment with the
Virek boy — less symbolic, more practical-feeling on the surface.
His body was sufficient. The armor was a statement of what he was
made to be, and he had just started to understand that he didn't
know yet what he *was*. The Glaive he had already left at the brook; this was the quieter letting-go. The armor was just equipment. He set it down
somewhere — a cache, a ruin, a location he may or may not remember
— and walked away.

He does not have it by the time he meets Adura. He does not have
it by Book 1. The Era_2.md line "he started looking for his armor"
refers to the Blank Years, when Boltcaster bolts began piercing his
hide in ways pre-Titansfall weapons never did — but by that point
he had been without it long enough that going back felt like a
retreat into something he had already walked away from. He looked
for alternatives. Hexwood scale armor — or, later in the search,
Keth-made Serpent Scale specifically, since that craft exists well
before the Blank Years begin — is the best available equivalent: a
different, unrelated technology that happens to solve the same
problem by similar mechanical means, arrived at twice,
independently, by makers who never met. Whether he has that by
Book 1 is a drafting decision.

What is locked: the original armor exists somewhere on Anka,
unclaimed, unrecognized, worth an extraordinary fortune to anyone
who knew what they were looking at. Third is the only living being
who would recognize it on sight. He has not gone back for it.

**Third's batteries — Titan-original containment (REVISED, not
Charter technology):**

Third does not run on Powder Charter magefire technology. He runs
on two Titan-original containment vessels, roughly pickle-jar
sized, in his chest, which the
Titan willed into existence to hold and transfer its own essence.
These predate the Charter, predate the Keth's discovery of Ptah,
predate the concept of a magefire battery entirely. The Charter's
batteries are the copy. Third's are the original. Neither party
knows the relationship.

What this means practically:
  - Third cannot buy replacements. What he needs is not
    commercially available and never has been.
  - He CAN drain Charter-standard magefire jars into his own
    system. The energy is compatible even if the vessel is not —
    close enough approximation that it sustains him, the way a
    Boltcaster bolt's Ptah charge can jump-start something that runs on
    Titan essence. Not efficient, not elegant. It works. The
    image: he holds a jar to his chest or forearm, and his body 'adjusts' a bit to connect, then
    drains it. No high. No surge. Just the lights staying on. He
    has been doing this for ~300,000 spins. Maintenance, not
    enhancement.
  - He replenishes roughly one vessel every ~500 spins. This has
    always required scrounging, buying, or trading — a constant
    background fact of his existence.
  - mathematically : takes about 3 magefire jars to replenish one of his Titanic vessels.

Lujian's sourcing problem (Era 2) is resolved by this: pre-Charter,
Keth traded magefire openly through established routes.
Lujian, a former scholar with knowledge of old trade paths, spent
everything she had acquiring magefire jars through those routes.
She was not buying replacement vessels — she was buying fuel. The
closest approximation. It worked.

**LANDSCAPE & THE TODDLER-GOD AESTHETIC (locked — apply on every
travel passage):**

The Titan had a god's power and a child's mind. Anka is not
designed; it is STREWN — a playroom after the child was dragged
away. Default register for all countryside: MASSIVE, and
beautiful-and-frightening BY TURN (the same feature is wondrous on
approach and lethal up close). The world should never read
"normal" once the party is outside a settlement. Grammar of the
wildness (use, don't list):
  - TOO BIG: things at a scale no purpose explains (the Causeway,
    the Spire, the Splinters). Awe first, dread second.
  - ABANDONED MID-THOUGHT: structures/landforms started and left
    (a wall going nowhere, a road to no front, a mountain
    half-made). The child lost interest.
  - OBSESSIVE REPETITION: the same shape made over and over (Snake
    Forest's identical bends; ranks of identical splinters; fields
    of identical chimneys). Sane things vary; made-by-a-fixation
    things don't.
  - DRAGGED / GOUGED: the land scored as if something vast was
    hauled across it -> "TITAN FOOTPRINTS" (below).
  - BEAUTIFUL-THEN-LETHAL: the turn is mandatory. Shard canopy
    glitters, then the leaves fall and stab; the warm plain ends
    at a blizzard wall; a green glade is a Bog.

SHARD TREES (taller, wilder): grow like bamboo (~a thumb-length/
day), but RIDICULOUSLY TALL — slender bone-pale trunks shooting far
overhead, bare for most of their height, amber glass-leaf foliage
bundled in a BURST AT THE CROWN. A mature shard stand reads like a
cathedral of pale masts with a glittering amber roof you can't
reach. Wind in the crowns = the tinkling-glass sound raining down;
the higher the wind, the more razor leaves break free and fall —
danger comes FROM ABOVE in a shard stand.

TITAN FOOTPRINTS: great gouged scars/troughs and shield-rock

was hauled (or walked, or thrown) across the world. Roads and
trails thread THROUGH them. In-world name: "Titan footprints" / "the knuckles." Treated as ordinary travel terrain
by locals; lands on the reader as awe + unease. (Distinct from the
Giant's Graveyard splinters and the black Ptah cracks.)

SENSELESS WEATHER (locked): weather on Anka does not obey sane
rules. Fronts can STAND STILL, move in discrete WALLS, or end at a
hard line. Travellers routinely see weather as a WALL ahead and
step into/through it. In-world narrators treat this as ordinary,
never as strange-relative-to-another-world. Use sparingly; do not
over-explain the mechanism (there is none; the Titan made it and

# PART FIVE — PEOPLES

**Humins:** Primary people of Anka in the current era. Origin
contested even among greybeards — some traditions hold the Giants
made them, others that the Titans reforged Humins out of the
Virek, or out of the Giants, after the fact.

**Hin:** Memetic origin. A tale persisting long enough, or a
collaborative sculpture of sufficient shared intention, becomes
real. Every Hin is a story that survived. Not created by Giants.
BARRED FROM AKNA (locked) — see "Where Stories Go to Die" above.
No Hin can ever be in the grey and remain.

**Kro (Elves):** Born from the Virek's final, self-inflicted
ritual — the pale-skinned, "light" half. Pale, fine-featured,
emotionally restrained. Kept the ancestral memories; rational half
of the split. Elves and Orks share a deep, mutual hatred everyone
is aware of and almost no one can explain — the truth (two halves
of the same severed whole) is not common knowledge.

**Goblins, Orks & Ogres (Ork):** Goblins are small, vicious, green

the stages are connected. This entire lineage is what became of

splitting ritual.

**The Forged:** Created by the Titan under the mountain as an army
of attack-drones, deliberately built to mock the Virek's
appearance even as they were sent to destroy them. Full
description in Part Three above. Awakened (gained genuine
self-awareness) at Titansfall, when the last Titan fell and its
dust touched the battlefield. Most couldn't cope and retreated
into long torpor. In the current era, a few have begun stirring
again. Memory of Virek, Humins, Hin; Kro and Ork are strange to
them; Giants are myth. Third is unique — the only known Akna
side-stepper.

Forged regard them as little more than rumor and legend. Status
unknown. Actually Cyclopskin.

**Emperor** over the Giants — the only intelligent beings on Anka

**Keth (relocated from Bestiary to Kindred & Peoples — see

from a mix of Humins and serpents, though the Keth themselves
rarely confirm or deny it. Keth society is rigidly caste-based:
each city is led by a Daimyo, served by life-loyal soldier-retainers

armor of exceptional, almost ceremonial quality. "Witch" druids are
common among the adult population; larger settlements support
sorcerers as well — magical talent and political power tend to go
hand in hand among the Keth, who are famously, almost obsessively
devoted to mastering magic wherever they can get access to it.

What's less known: Keth crafters hold the only working knowledge
of how raw Ptah becomes magefire — a closely guarded secret that,
over centuries, has made them quietly indispensable to nearly
everything on Anka that flies, fires a Boltcaster, or lights a battery.
Few outside the caste structure ever see how that knowledge is
actually used, or how far it reaches.

"Scalykind" persists only as an old, blunt Humin field term —
usable in dialogue or outsider narration, never as Keth
self-naming and never in this World Bible's own reference voice.

# PART SIX — CHARACTERS

**Third:** ~300,000 spins. Awakened at Titansfall. Full physical
description in Part Three. See name origin below.

**Third — name origin:** Named by Adura, early in their road
together (~spin 268,000). Her uncle was a woodsman named Third —
taught her forest survival from childhood, killed not by violence
but by disease carried by a Herdukii pack moving through his
forest during the Pale and Green War, near Luek. She told Third

The name is part of what makes him *there*. Before it, he is

Adura notices it retrospectively, writing from memory in the early
slat-book entries: *he was already himself when I found him, but
he didn't know it yet.*

NOT HIS FIRST NAME (see "Liwon — naming" and "Lujian," below, for

without saying who gave it or what it was. She doesn't press. This
means Adura's gift isn't overwriting anything; she's the first

**Liwon — naming:** A reciprocal naming loop, established in

Adura's by roughly 267,000 spins.

At the Transformation — Liwon's sundering into two dying children,

# PART EIGHT — MATERIALS & ECONOMY

## PTAH — THE UNIFIED SUBSTANCE (locked cosmology)

ONE substance, the Titan's blood, in different states and grades.
  - WET Titan blood (red, going black): wells from geysers; while
    wet it anoints wood -> HEXWOOD (keeps a red, mahogany-like sheen). Imbibed,
    causes rage or incapacitation.
  - DRIED Titan blood = PTAH: blue-white, clear, GLASSLIKE. Cannot
    be reused as blood. This is the feedstock of all magic-tech on
    Anka.

HEXWOOD IS POST-TITANSFALL ONLY (locked): Geysers — where Titan
blood reaches the surface — are a post-Titansfall phenomenon. The
blood wells up because the Titan is dying/fragmented; a living
Titan's blood was contained within its body. No geysers existed
before Titansfall, therefore no accessible wet Titan blood,
therefore no Hexwood. Pre-Titansfall weapons were jade, stone, and
bone only. Hexwood emerged as a material in the generations after
Titansfall as geysers opened and people discovered what the pooled
blood did to wood. This is a locked principle — do not place
Hexwood in pre-Titansfall contexts, including Third's original
armor or the Virek's weapons during the Titan war.

"BLACK GLASS" IS A TRICK OF LIGHT: Ptah is blue-white/clear when
thin; thick masses and broad swaths read BLACK by depth (like deep
clear water/glass). The Glass Sea and the radiating cracks/veins
are all Ptah deposits — same substance, just bulk. The public has
never connected any of this to the Titan's blood; "black glass" is
just a material found near old battlefields and dead geyser-fields.
Turning raw Ptah into usable magefire is a KETH secret — what they
actually do to it is unknown to outsiders, and grinding is
specifically NOT it; there is no public process at all. (SECRET —
both "Ptah = dried Titan blood" AND the Keth process stay hidden
from characters/reader until the chosen reveal.)

TWO GRADES:
  - ANKA-RESIDUE PTAH: softer — geyser crust, the cracks, the
    Glass Sea. The grade Keth take as raw material for magefire.
  - TITAN-CREATED PTAH: harder, denser, MADE not dried-residue.
    The Spire is this — the oldest and purest Ptah anywhere, gone
    adamant. Unbreakable; nothing has ever been usefully taken
    from it.

## GEYSER LIFECYCLE

Geysers are CONTINUOUS SPRAYS that build tapering chimneys
(termite-mound shape) ~2–3 humans high; the spray dries in air to
blue-white Ptah crust on the outside of the tower. The pools near them 
are always Spire-side of the tower, and roughly half a (or hin's/child's) stone throw across.
  - The spray FALLS AND POOLS at the base. People collect the WET
    blood by dipping from the pool — NOT by standing in the spray
    (wet blood on skin triggers the rage). Working people gather
    at the pools, downwind of the spray.
  - The wet pool feeds bizarre, often dangerous flora ringing the
    geyser. Must be CUT BACK before a pool can be safely worked —
    an untended free geyser chokes itself in deadly growth.
    (Third's neutrality-task: clear the flora so the poor can
    harvest free.)
  - DEATH: when the vent seals, the site goes dry, the pools dry
    too, turning BLACK. The blackened pools and runoff LINK UP
    across Anka, vein to vein, into the cracks and ultimately the
    Glass Sea. The black cracks/veins/sea are the DRIED CORPSES of
    old geyser-fields — Anka's scars are a circulatory system of a
    long-dead bleeding; every crack was once a living pool.

Sites are on a clock; Third has kept a SUCCESSION of sites free
across his long life, watching each seal over, moving to the next.

## THE GEYSER COUNCILS — REVISED INTO "THE DUSTIES"

Retire the earlier framing of Geyser Councils as small, reactive,
purely local bodies squeezed between the Powder Charter and free
harvesters. They are not squeezed. **They are the Powder Charter's
upstream half** — the part of the operation that actually knows
where the blood is going.

Formal name: **The Powder Charter** (unchanged — the legal/
commercial entity, the one that licenses Boltcaster ammunition and
battery supply rim-wide).

Colloquial name: **The Dusties.** Used for the operational arm
specifically — the people who physically find, claim, work, and
seal geyser sites. Slightly less contemptuous than "the
Dustkeepers" (rim-wide commercial slang for the Charter as a
whole); "Dusties" is closer to "the miners" or "the prospectors"
in register — distinct from the institution that profits from it.

**What the Dusties actually do:**

Not bureaucrats reacting to geysers as they appear — a standing
intelligence and logistics operation built around accumulated
**clockwork mathematics**: generations of recorded vent behavior,
pressure cycles, seal timing, letting them predict when an active
geyser will die and roughly where the next will open. This is
their actual product. The magefire trade is downstream of it.

Operational cycle:
1. **Predict.** Using the accumulated mathematics (treated
   internally as a precise, almost ritual discipline), forecast a
   site's remaining lifespan and scout likely new locations before
   they manifest.
2. **Arrive first.** A Dusty crew reaches a newly active or
   about-to-seal site ahead of local settlements, geyser councils,
   or competing claimants.
3. **Seal off and charge admission.** Their primary local revenue
   is access — controlling who may approach a working geyser, at
   what rate, under what terms. The folksy wet-blood anointing
   trade survives only at the Dusties' sufferance and usually at a
   toll. In-world average is about a black per sword-sized item.
4. **Withdraw on schedule.** When their own mathematics say a site
   is nearing its seal, they pull out cleanly, citing the closure
   as confirmation of their expertise. Builds their reputation for
   omniscience — itself a political asset.

**The Third wrinkle:** Third's neutrality-task across his long
life directly undercuts the Dusties' toll model at any site he
chooses to work. He doesn't do this as opposition; he does it
because standing still agrees with the liar and the work is in
front of him. The Dusties have never been able to build a
permanent claim on something that goes dry, and a 300,000-spin-old
being who simply outlasts their patience at any given site,
repeatedly, across centuries, is the closest thing they have to a
natural predator. They likely have institutional memory of "the
wooden one" without any coherent theory of what he is or why he
keeps doing this.

## KETH <-> DUSTIES — THE SUPPLY RELATIONSHIP

The Keth do not buy raw Ptah from the Dusties as a straightforward
commercial transaction. The relationship runs on two parallel,
mostly invisible channels — both of which leave the Dusties
believing they hold the upper hand.

**Channel one — clean intermediaries.** While a site is still
active and under Dusty control, Keth buyers never appear in
person. Humin or Kro middlemen — traders who believe they're
sourcing an ordinary, if oddly regulated, raw material — purchase
dried Ptah margin-stock from Dusty-controlled sites through
ordinary-looking trade channels. The middlemen don't know what
they're carrying or why it commands the price it does; they have
no idea they're Keth agents. This is the legal, visible half of the
supply chain, and it's the half the Dustkeepers/Powder Charter
believes constitutes the whole relationship.

**Channel two — the sealed sites.** The Dusties' own mathematics
tell them precisely when a site is dead. They file it, withdraw,
move to the next prediction. What they don't know — and have never
had reason to suspect, since their expertise is in timing, not
material science — is that a "dead" site still holds dried Ptah
the Keth can use, in forms and quantities the Dusties' instruments
don't register as valuable. Keth crews (or Keth-directed non-Keth
labor, to avoid drawing attention to Keth presence at a supposedly
worthless site) move in quietly after Dusty withdrawal and extract
what's actually still there.

**The con, stated plainly:** The Keth do not sell finished magefire
back to the Dusties. The Dusties have no need for magefire as an
institution — prospecting and toll, not consumption. The buyer of
the finished product is the Powder Charter's **separate
distribution and licensing arm** — the part of the institution
that sets prices and controls rim-wide supply of batteries and
Boltcaster bolts. The Dusties are only one arm of that same overall
Charter — the upstream, extraction-and-claims arm.

This means the institution is, without realizing it, paying twice
for its own resource: the Dusties' arm sells raw Ptah margin-stock
cheap to clean intermediaries, with no idea those traders are
Keth-directed; the Charter's licensing arm then buys the finished,
marked-up magefire from the Keth at full price for rim-wide
distribution, with no idea the raw material in that magefire
originated in its own claimed sites. Two divisions of the same
institution, fully siloed, each transacting honestly within its own
silo — and the Keth sitting precisely in the gap between them,
profiting from both ends of a loop the institution can't see is a
loop at all.

The Dusties' real expertise — clockwork prediction of geological
timing — is genuine and valuable. Their belief that this expertise
gives them meaningful control over where the material's value
ultimately ends up is the blind spot.

**Why this holds together politically:** Nobody currently has cause
to expose it. The Dusties see traders, not Keth, and dead ground,
not continued extraction. The licensing arm sees a Keth supplier
delivering a contracted product at a contracted price and has no
operational reason to audit where Keth source their raw material —
specifically the part of the arrangement the Keth have always kept
opaque, citing craft secrecy. The Keth have no incentive to correct
anyone's picture: an institution that doesn't know it's both their
supplier and their customer is far easier to work than one that's
noticed.

**Narrative utility:** A ready-made political thriller thread for
Book 2/3 or later — a Dusty administrator who starts to suspect
the "dead site" extractions, or a Keth Daimyo who decides the
clean-intermediary channel has become a liability, would each
independently destabilize a system that has run quietly for
centuries. Third, who has watched individual Dusty sites rise and
fall without ever seeing the larger pattern, would be a uniquely
positioned *accidental witness* if he ever connected what he's
seen across enough sites and enough centuries — a structural echo
of Book 2's artifact-pattern beat (one data point is not a
pattern; he files it and walks).

## STANDARDS GUILD = THE POWDER CHARTER (naming unified)

These are **one institution at two points in its history**, not
two separate bodies. "Standards Guild" is the OLDER name,
reflecting the institution's original function; "the Powder
Charter" (colloquially "the Dustkeepers") is its CURRENT name,
reflecting what it actually became.

**The history, briefly:** Before the monopoly, the body that
became the Powder Charter genuinely did what its old name says —
set and enforced *standards*: cargo dimensions, caliber
consistency, construction tolerances, the unglamorous
infrastructure work that makes a rim-wide trade economy function
at all. Real, useful, broadly uncontroversial work, and the reason
the institution had the reach and legitimacy to end up controlling
magefire in the first place — it was already the body everyone
went through to standardize anything.

Magefire control arrived as mission creep: a standards body that
already certified batteries, bolts, and skybarge cargo specs was
perfectly positioned to become the exclusive certifying — and then
exclusive supplying — body once Keth manufacture started flowing
through it at scale. By the time anyone might have objected, "the
Standards Guild" wasn't describing what the institution mostly did
anymore. The new name caught up to the new reality.

**Usage going forward:**
  - **"The Powder Charter"** — current, formal, rim-wide name.
    Use in present-day narration (Book 1, Book 2's framing
    relative to its own era) without qualification once the
    institution has consolidated.
  - **"The Standards Guild"** — retained as the institution's
    HISTORICAL name. Usable in: older characters' habitual speech,
    historical/backstory references, or scenes set far enough in
    the past that the rename hadn't happened yet. At the time of
    Book 2's Era 2 events (~268,000–299,000 spins), the
    institution is STILL LOCAL — a single city or region — so
    "Standards Guild" or simply an unnamed regional authority is
    the correct register for that era; "Powder Charter" as a
    RIM-WIDE entity is a development Third watches happen across
    the centuries between Book 2 and Book 1.
  - **"The Dustkeepers"** — current colloquial/slightly
    contemptuous trade slang for the Powder Charter as a whole.
  - **"The Dusties"** — the Charter's operational/prospecting arm
    specifically (see above). NOT interchangeable with
    "Dustkeepers."

This also sharpens the Dusties material above: the Dusties'
operational ancestor-function — standardizing claims, timing,
access — is the same impulse that built the original Standards
Guild. The Charter's entire DNA, from its oldest function to its
current monopoly, is the same instinct: control the rules everyone
else has to play by, and the actual material control follows
naturally from there.

## TITAN-WILLED MATERIALS

A distinct category, separate from crafted or natural materials.
Form imposed by Titan will without mechanical or biological
underpinning — the Titan decided something would behave a certain
way, and it does, regardless of what it's actually made of.

Instances: the Forged's leather-vine musculature (willed to look
like muscle); the Forged's clockwork interior (willed to animate,
not mechanically load-bearing); Third's containment vessels
(willed to hold and transfer essence); Titanic artefacts
incorporating materials that behave like jade wire, stone, or cord
without being those things.

Third can recognize Titan-willed material because his own body is
made of it. The recognition is not intellectual — it is the same
as recognizing your own handwriting on a stranger's page. He may
not be able to explain what he's recognized or why. He goes still.
Adura learned to write down the stillnesses.

## KETH AND THE SILK ANALOGY

Keth discovered the Titan's containment-vessel principle by
studying Forged recovered from Titansfall battlefields — not Third
specifically, but others, less intact. They could not replicate
the Titan-willed containment or the original essence. What they
discovered was that ground Ptah in a glass jar with jade wire
produced a similar enough energy signature to be useful. They
aimed for a power source. They got magefire.

They used it for weapons first, because the war was still ongoing
and that was what was needed — the Chinese-finding-gunpowder
parallel: the thing they found first was fireworks; the thing
history remembers is cannon. The Keth found a power source and
built a weapons industry.

They hid the manufacture of both batteries and Boltcasters the way the
Chinese hid silk — knowledge as monopoly. Boltcasters, however, are
mechanically simple: a sealed tube, a water chamber, a trigger.
Once enough people had seen one, reverse engineering was
inevitable. The Boltcaster proliferated. The powder did not. The Keth
retained the powder monopoly; the Charter later licensed and
consolidated it.

## CORE MATERIAL PRINCIPLE — NO STEEL, NO IRON

Anka has no iron metallurgy. There is no steel, and no iron to
make it from. The world's tagline holds literally: "no gold
pieces, no dragons, no gods and no steel." Two materials fill the
role steel plays elsewhere:
  - HEXWOOD — wood carved then anointed with Titan blood, harder
    than stone. Premium hard material: weapons, tools,
    heirloom-grade needles, lock-boxes. Bone is the cheap
    substitute it's contrasted against. After roughly a thousand
    spins, the anointing fades and the item needs re-anointing.
    Before the Titans fell, their own minions carried something
    that looked like Hexwood but was actually Titan-reinforced
    Jade. When the Titans died, that reinforcement died with them.
    The handful of cases since where someone managed to magically
    reinforce such an item again are where the word "artifact"
    comes from.
  - JADE — a greenish stone, the color of oxidized copper, with
    the ductility of copper; workable, but soft, needing constant
    maintenance. Not copper, and does not behave like it
    chemically or metallurgically — resemblance is color and
    workability only.
    Anointed with Titan blood it becomes poisonous GREENSTEEL
    (a NAME, not actual steel) — a paralytic with no known
    antidote, no harder than the jade it was carved from. The
    popular belief that anointing ALSO hardens jade (or copper) is
    FALSE — it only poisons. Few who've tested the claim are eager
    to correct it.

POV NOTE: in-world narrators (e.g. Tasha, Adura) never describe
Anka by what it lacks relative to worlds they've never seen — no
references to "steel," "stars," or "moons" as absences. They name
what IS. "Steel" may appear only in the compound name
"Greensteel."

## MAGEFIRE, BOLTCASTERS, AND BATTERIES

Magefire: the refined product Keth make from raw Ptah — process
unknown to outsiders. Feeds Boltcasters and (for Third
specifically) is drained as approximation fuel, though his own
containment is Titan-original, not Charter technology (see Part
Three). SECRET: both what Ptah is (dried Titan blood) and the Keth
process, guarded by the guild/Charter and Keth separately.

Boltcasters: the weapon itself (sealed tube + water chamber) is simple
to make. The bolt — magefire + water -> steam-and-lightning burst,
no projectile — is the real secret: Keth manufacture only, sold
through the Powder Charter, which sets prices and supply.

**BOLTCASTERS — TIMELINE (locked):** Boltcasters are NOT new technology at
the time of Book 2. They began appearing during Third's torpor
(~1,220 spins onward) and are well-established by the time he
meets Adura (~268,000 spins). By the Dorn era (~293,000), they are
ubiquitous — unremarkable as objects. Dorn's Boltcaster is not notable
as a weapon type; what's notable is the shot. [FLAG: remove any
implication in Book 1 or Book 2 drafts that Boltcasters are new or
remarkable as a category. The weapon is ordinary; skill with it is
not.]

The "new arrows" that begin hurting Third in the late Blank years
are Boltcaster bolts or Boltcaster-adjacent projectiles — they carry a
magefire discharge that interacts badly with Third's wooden body
in ways stone or even Hexwood-tipped arrows never did. Pre-
Titansfall materials hurt him the way they always did. Post-Boltcaster
materials hurt him differently. He started looking for his armor.

**KERN'S BOW (Book 1 — flag):** If Boltcasters proliferated from
~1,220 spins onward and became dominant by ~50,000–100,000 spins,
then by Book 1's present day (~300,000 spins), traditional archery
is an archaism — ~200,000 spins out of common use. A Kro elf
maintaining traditional bowcraft is a deliberate cultural or
political statement: a Kro identity marker (we do not depend on
Charter powder), a rejection of Charter-dependent technology, or
both. Kern's bow surviving 300,000 spins and still carrying
meaning is stranger and more earned than previously framed — not
just an old weapon, but the last of a form the Charter made
obsolete.

Magefire batteries: powdered Ptah in Ptah-glass jars, jade wires
inside. Power magic, and (racked in large numbers) the fans that
keep a skybarge aloft. Sougad: SIX, grafted to his back, powering
both his magic and his own floatation (personal effect, distinct
from skybarge lift). Third: TWO, Titan-original, NOT
Charter-manufactured — see Part Three for full distinction.

Titan Bones: floatstone — small chips repelled by black glass,
hence they float. A Gnawing Fields curiosity only, harvested and
sold as novelties/charms by a rim-town economy. Does NOT power
skybarges (fans + large arcane batteries do that). Any attempt to 
put weight on them such as to utilize them for lift, fails, causing the rocks to fall.

Coinage: Blacks (silver, universally tarnished black by ambient
magefire dust) and Teeth (wooden tricorn). Higher denominations
are barter. One Black is about 10 Teeth. Blacks are verified by 
scratching the tarnish (to verify material), then weight. Teeth are 
verified by the name/resemblance on their face... magically verified
if needed.

Charter standardizes Blacks outright (real bullion value, worth
the certification effort). For Teeth, the Charter doesn't
standardize the face — it standardizes the blank (Tooth-grade
wood, certified for grade/tolerance/cut via the Hexwood
Supplement) and the press (consistent strike depth/pattern across
certified presses), and leaves the face entirely local, made in
the resemblance of the local highest authority/ruler. A Tooth is
NOT hexwood at the point of stamping — it's struck as ordinary,
uncured wood (carpenters paid well for selecting stock that will
anoint evenly and hold a clean strike). Only after stamping is it
anointed with wet Titan blood — the actual mint act, and the
moment it hardens into true Hexwood: mahogany-red, effectively
unalterable. Before anointing a strike could be re-cut; after, it
can't be without destroying the coin. Like all Hexwood, the
anointing fades after roughly a thousand spins without renewal —
old, un-reanointed Teeth soften back to ordinary wood and age out
of hard currency on their own, no institutional recall needed.

Transport: no mounts. Single-carts background only. Skybarges
(fans + arcane batteries) for long distance. Single-carts are very similar 
to earth "rickshaw" carts (one wheel instead of two, however), pulled by 
a Humin, or small group of goats or dogs, or pulled along by a small clockwork 
driven pair of wheels. The "Clockwheels" are prestigious, but prone to error and
expensive to repair/maintain. Some Hin wagon-homes are Clockwheel driven, some
Hin or animal pulled (often a dozen dogs / goats). Large enough Single-carts
 or wagons are designed to carry standardized containers (3x3x9), tho most Hin 
carts unconsciously rebel at this, to the frustration of the Dusties and no one is 
sure why.

Three-tier trade system: Skybarges carry the fast, high-value, and
speculative — no fixed schedule, no guaranteed buyer; Waj Hool's
independence is the model, not the exception. The Charter touches
every tier nominally (cargo dimensions, mill/currency
certification) but that reach rarely helps an average person —
mostly fees and inspection, not service. Ground caravans carry the
slow, low-value, high-volume staples — grain, ordinary materials,
livestock — not worth a skybarge's gamble and not needing the
Charter's blessing to move.

Roads are managed by whoever uses them — no unified network, no
central maintenance authority. Road condition is a legible signal
of relationship health: roads near a city-state are well-kept by
that city-state's own labor and interest; roads connecting Town
Compact members are a compact obligation, and their condition
signals whether the compact still holds; roads between ordinary,
non-compact settlements are informal and can lapse without
warning; everywhere else reverts to track, rut, and ford. Third,
after 300,000 spins, reads the state of the world in road
condition alone. This sharpens the Causeway's uncanniness by
contrast — every other road on Anka visibly belongs to someone,
however loosely, and the Causeway belongs to no one.

Caravaneering is family- and community-run, inherited rather than
guild-organized — deliberate contrast to the informal-but-
collective Skybarge Masters. Hin families are disproportionately
represented among caravaneers: Hin are, at root, stories that
survived being told, and a people whose own nature depends on
continued telling are naturally suited to work that carries both
goods and news between towns. Their gift-giving custom already
reads as a caravaneer's habit. (Narrative reserve, not locked: a
Hin route falling out of use — going quiet, no longer told — sits
close to something already dangerous for Hin specifically, given
Akna is where forgotten stories go and don't return.)

Postal delivery is not a separate system — mail rides skybarges
and caravans as ordinary cargo, handed to whoever's headed the
right way; no guild, no fixed rate, no guaranteed delivery time.
This gives an approaching skybarge or caravan a double meaning for
a watching settlement: Charter business (inspection, fees,
generally unwelcome) or news and letters from somewhere else
(personal, hoped-for) — nobody knows which until the operator is
close enough to hail.

Glassvine: grows over stationary objects, restraining anything
left still too long under a thickening wrap; stems packed with
glass-like splinters, so cutting something free releases them in a
shattering spray.

## MILLS

Three power tiers, roughly mapping to a settlement's geography and
Charter dependency: **water mills** (baseline, free, no Charter
relationship — river towns are quietly more independent for this
reason); **vertical windmills**, clockwork style, not the broad
canvas-sailed variety — gear-and-ratchet vertical-axis rotors in
the same register as Boltcasters and Dusty geyser mathematics, the
inland standard; **arcane battery mills**, expensive, used where
water/wind are unreliable, requiring a standing Charter resupply
contract the Charter knowingly prices as leverage.

Charter mill certification is among the Standards Guild's oldest
functions, predating magefire — part of why the institution had
the reach to absorb the magefire trade later. Certified size scales
to registered population with a mandatory **growth reserve**: a
future expansion site identified, left unbuilt, and filed in
Charter cartographic record — cannot be built on by a third party
without Charter approval, meaning a growing town must return to the
Charter (and pay) to expand. Rough tiers: under 200 registered =
single wheel (grain + fulling); 200–800 = two wheels (grain +
secondary use); 800–2,000 = three wheels (grain, fulling, one
specialist wheel); above 2,000 = bespoke, on-site inspector, no
formula.

Secondary/specialist certified uses: fulling (cloth), oil pressing,
sawmilling (Hexwood requires its own slow, expensive Hexwood
Supplement certification — a real bottleneck for forest
settlements), ore/stamp crushing (Keth city-states hold their own,
refuse Charter inspection — unresolved tension), trip-hammer forges
(one of the oldest certified uses), paper/pulp mills (Charter is
both primary customer and certifying body for these — a circularity
it doesn't find troubling). Battery mills additionally require a
registered resupply contract; lapsed agreements technically void
certification, enforcement inconsistent in remote areas.

A Charter mill certificate is a fast worldbuilding signal: current
multi-wheel = stable and Charter-connected; single-wheel/expired =
young, poor, remote, or deliberately outside Charter reach; none at
all = pre-Charter, off the map, or hiding on purpose. The **mill
inspector** — long-posted, usually the most locally knowledgeable
Charter employee around — is a useful minor character type for
grounding Charter politics without a major institutional actor.

## FOG-WICKS

Household water harvesting in fog-prone regions — folk technology,
NOT a Charter system, deliberately outside Charter reach. A length
of bone (joint- or long-bone), staked upright in a shallow basin,
tipped with a raw Ptah sliver, set high on a sill or plinth (out of
a child's reach, above whatever moves at ground level after dark).
Primed and lit at dusk, it draws fog through the night by an
unexplained mechanism — same category of strangeness as the
Causeway; do not resolve institutionally — leaving standing water
by morning. To an unfamiliar traveler, a street of them at dusk
reads uncomfortably like household shrines; to residents it's no
stranger than banking a hearth.

The Ptah sliver is a consumable, needing regular replacement —
making every fog-wick household a small, unwitting customer of the
Ptah trade, the first point where an ordinary Humin household
touches the same supply chain feeding Boltcasters and batteries. Dusties
sell graded "Prime"/"Choice" slivers at a premium, marketed as more
effective; any raw sliver actually works identically — a
street-level echo of the Dusties' institutional-scale reputation-
leveraging. Wealth shows in the wick's mount, not its fuel: plain
wood bowl on a stump vs. a carved bone or hexwood plinth, sometimes
family-motif carved. Scope: household drinking water only, in
fog-prone regions — does not address agriculture, livestock,
industrial use, or non-fog regions.

## POWDER CHARTER STANDARDIZATIONS (beyond magefire)

Things the Charter enforces rim-wide that appear to serve the
public and serve the Charter more. Not directly touched in Book 1,
but live, knowable canon:

**Cargo dimensions (3m × 3m × 9m):** all carts, crates, and
containers built to Skybarge transfer specifications. Every town's
economy is quietly built around Skybarge access, which the Charter
controls. Towns that can't afford Skybarge rates are still
building Charter-spec containers because nothing else fits the
loading frames.

**Boltcaster caliber standardization:** one ammunition size, one jar
format, one licensed supplier per region. Every Boltcaster-owner is on
a permanent powder dependency. Exception: Skybarge batteries,
which require a larger format the Charter maintains through a
separate, less-publicized supply chain — never standardized
because standardizing it would invite competition. While it seems 
the Charter is enforcing the Magefire standardization, its actually 
because the Keth will not make nor offer any other sizes. It is simply
an unchangable fact, and ignore when the Dusties bring it up.

**Sougad's jars as implicit conflict:** Sougad's batteries are
non-standard — custom, pre-Charter, Titan-adjacent in origin. The
Charter has no record of them and no authority over them. Nobody
at the Charter knows why the non-standard jars exist or who made
them; officially an anomaly too infrequent and too frightening to
pursue. Third's own vessels are similarly non-standard and
similarly ignored — the Charter has tried to bring him into
compliance, generated paperwork, and nothing else happened.

**Town plinths and maps:** every new town is required to install a
founding plinth — Charter-standard dimensions, naming the founding
date and the founding dead. Maps are updated using the Spire as
fixed reference point. The Charter controls cartography. Towns
that predate the Charter, or that fell before documentation, exist
in official limbo — present on the land, absent from the record.
Third has watched settlements vanish from maps because no Charter
representative was present to write them down.

## SLAT-BOOKS

The standard writing medium of Anka. Each slat is page-height but
single-character wide — writing is vertical, one character column
per slat. Slats are bound together with string and either unfold
accordion-style (WWWW viewed from above) or roll, depending on the
maker and intended use. Individual slats can be removed, replaced,
or reordered — a slat-book is not fixed the way a bound paper book
would be, which makes them practical for ledgers, contracts, and
any document that needs updating.

Writing methods: by hand (brush and ink) or pressed. Slats are
cheap and common trade goods; the writing materials are more
valuable than the medium. A slat-book intended for archive use may
be oiled or treated; working copies are plain.

The last slat of a filled book has binding holes along one edge
for the next slat that was never added.

## CLOCKWORK PRESS

A mechanical device for pressing text onto slat surfaces. Works by
locking individual type-slats (single-character-wide, matching the
slat-book format) into a cylindrical frame and pressing via rolling a 
slat through it, so that the whole face is pressed against
the writing surface. Produces clean, repeatable
impressions; allows individual sentence-slats to be swapped in and
out without resetting the whole frame — the "clock" part refers to
the precision mechanism and power, not to timekeeping.

Expensive, rare, available in larger towns with sufficient craft
infrastructure. Owning one marks a serious commitment to producing
written material in volume rather than relying on hand-copying. A
single press is a significant purchase even for someone with
accumulated Blacks.

Third buys Adura one during her settled period — an extravagant,
slightly awkward gift from a being who has money and no intuition
for what to spend it on, prompted by an early offhand remark she
made on the road about recording their travels someday. The gift
is the press and a supply of blank slats: the whole system.

## LEGAL & DISPUTE INFRASTRUCTURE

Law on Anka is inherently local — no central government, no
rim-wide court. Each town/city-state has its own rules and
arbiters, "generally similar" across Anka without central
enforcement. Two forces explain this, working together: a **shared
but forgotten root** (Acnash's giant-empire era — see Part Five —
diluted across Virek → Kro/Ork/Humins descent over 300,000+ spins,
surviving now only as unconscious inheritance and a handful of
proper nouns like the Causeway's name) and ongoing **convergent
necessity** (trade requiring compatible-enough dispute norms).
Neither alone plausibly survives this much time intact; together,
they do.

The Charter deliberately avoids formal governmental responsibility
— governing means liability, a cost it has no reason to accept when
its actual leverage (supply, certification, currency) already gets
it what it wants. Local arbiters don't press cases against Dusty
personnel — not a written exemption (which a resentful town could
organize against) but an **unwritten understanding**: a remembered
consequence (resupply/certification quietly slowed, once, long ago,
somewhere) rather than a stated rule. No town wants to be the
lesson a second time.

This sharpens rather than resolves Book 2's skybarge jurisdiction
question: since Anka's entire legal framework is built on locality,
a skybarge belonging to no town is a structural hole the system was
never built to reach, not a mere oversight nobody's gotten around
to. Worth a character stating something close to this outright in
a Book 2 scene, if it fits.

# PART TEN — HISTORY

## THE PALE AND GREEN WAR

The formal name for what folk call by various regional terms. A
protracted, multi-decade conflict between Kro and Ork — the
sundered peoples, two halves of the Virek who do not know they are
two halves. Neither side would use the other's name for the war;
Humins named it after what they saw: pale-skinned Kro and
green-skinned Ork marching through their fields.

Folk names:
  - *The Long Grief* — generational suffering without naming
    sides; common in Humin settlements caught between armies.
    (LOCKED as primary folk usage.)
  - *The Old Wound* — implies it never fully healed; used in
    regions still feeling the aftermath.
  - *The Pale and Green War* — most widespread formal folk usage;
    accurate, neutral, requires no insider knowledge. (LOCKED as
    formal historical record.)

What most Humins don't know: the two sides are the same people.
The "deep enmity" nobody can explain is the echo of a
self-sundering. Third knows. Sougad knows. The Kro and Ork
themselves do not, or will not.

The war begins before Third's torpor and continues during it
(~1,220 onward). By the time Lujian wakes him (~100,000), the main
conflict has wound down, but its aftermath — displaced
populations, collapsed settlements, Herdukii packs ranging far
from their usual territory — continues for generations. Luek
survives the war but not indefinitely.

**THE KIN WARS (restricted knowledge):** the accurate name for the
Pale and Green War, known only to Third, Sougad, and possibly a
handful of Kro scholars with access to the oldest genealogies. Not
in public use. Neither side would recognize or accept it. Exists
as authorial backstory; does not appear in narration without heavy
framing.

## LUEK

A Humin nation, large enough to have flanks worth attacking, that
survived the Pale and Green War and continued for several thousand
spins afterward. In ruins by Book 1's present day (~300,000
spins) — declined or collapsed sometime after the war, cause
undecided. Adura's uncle died on its borderlands, defending
neutral forest against a Herdukii incursion local folk memory
reconstructed as a deliberate military flank attempt. It was
probably opportunistic displacement.

## HERDUKII AT LUEK

Family memory holds that Herdukii struck "the flanks of Luek" as a
deliberate tactical maneuver during the Pale and Green War. Almost
certainly not accurate. Herdukii are embodied rage, not soldiers —
they do not coordinate tactically. The war's chaos displaced packs
from their usual range; they attacked whatever was available.
Adura's uncle was a target of opportunity. The "flank attempt"
framing is a child's inherited retelling that flatters neither the
Herdukii nor the truth.

# PART ELEVEN — LANGUAGE & USAGE

**Rim-to-rim / rim-wide (house terms):** the world's equivalents
of "global." Anka is a flat disc; "rim-to-rim" spans its full
diameter; "rim-wide" is the casual form.
  - *Rim-to-rim* — formal or evocative usage. "A rim-to-rim
    monopoly." "Known rim-to-rim."
  - *Rim-wide* — casual speech. "The Dustkeepers are rim-wide
    now."

Neither "global" nor "worldwide" appears in narration or dialogue.
The Powder Charter becomes rim-wide between Book 2's era and Book
1. Third watched it happen.

Titanspeech glossary: see Part One.

# PART TWELVE — BOOK 2 MATERIAL (Adura's narration, Era 2)

## STRUCTURAL SUMMARY

**Narrator:** Adura. First person throughout. Chronological. No
braid.

**Act 1 (road, from memory):** Opens with her village, her
self-rescue, Third from the trees. The naming lands within Act 1
— early, chronologically, earning its weight through accumulation
rather than position. When she challenges him ("Blank isn't a
name"), he tells her, factually and without elaboration, that he
has been given a name before and does not use it — see "Third —
name origin" and "Liwon — naming," Part Six, for the full
mechanism. He does not say who gave it or what it was; she doesn't
press. The rest of the book is her account of who she named. Early
road together; she learns to read him from the outside. She begins
to drift toward settled life.

**Act 2 (journal):** The clockwork press arrives. The slat-book
proper begins — early entries reconstruct Act 1 from memory; the
journal then catches up and runs forward. Third visits with flat
accounts she translates. She brokers his contracts; meets always
outside of town.

  - *The map (Third's account, Dorn years):* brief, logistical. He
    crossed on a particular vessel. Unusual map, large, good
    craftsmanship. Not remarkable enough to dwell on. Adura
    records it as movement, not detail. No stillness, no flag.
  - *Sougad rumor (Dorn years):* see below.
  - *The map's loss (after Dorn's death):* Adura brings a contract
    opportunity — a skybarge crew seeking scouts and
    cartographers. She relays the detail: enormous map, stolen,
    always current, sounds like a tall tale but the coin is real.
    She's already on the contract terms. Third does the math. Goes
    still. She notices. Writes it down. Doesn't push. He doesn't
    tell her what connected.
  - *Dorn arrives (~two-thirds through):* the three travel
    actively together.

**Act 3 (losses, short):** Dorn's death arrives as an account she
has to write down. She includes the joke in full because she
doesn't want it to go unrecorded. Adura's illness closes the book
mid-thought. The last slat has binding holes along one edge.

**Coda:** Close-third, no narrator. A nervous villager delivers
the slat-book to Third at their usual meeting spot outside of town
— Adura's note, her first printed edition, her return gift for the
press. Third carries it. He walks. The Scar moves overhead. The
road continues. He gets smaller. The road bends. (Lonely Man
register. Short enough that it feels like the book ran out when
she did, and he just kept going anyway.)

## THE ARTIFACT

**Nature:** a navigation map, Titan-willed, hunter-aspect in
origin (Herduk's work — the Titan in its pursuit-and-tracking
fixation). Not attributable to a specific aspect by Third or
anyone else in the book; Third recognizes it as Titan-willed,
nothing more.

**Physical description:** approximately half the length of a
skybarge — enormous, closer to furniture or architectural feature
than portable instrument. Mounted flat or nearly flat in the
navigation area of the vessel. The surface is a material that
behaves like treated hide or fine vellum but is neither —
Titan-willed, non-reactive, non-degrading. The map shows Anka as
it currently is: settlements present and absent, roads open and
washed out, weather systems moving, terrain as it actually looks
today rather than when the map was made. It updates continuously.
Nobody on the crew thinks of this as remarkable — it has always
done this; they treat it the way you'd treat any good tool,
reliably, without curiosity about the mechanism.

**Custodianship:** a skybarge navigation crew, generational. The
map came with the vessel, or came to the vessel, long enough ago
that nobody knows its origin. It is simply *the map*. The largest
Humin-made maps are body-sized at most; this one, half a barge in
length, has always been explained as "the old builders made things
differently." Nobody has questioned this.

The navigator in Book 2 is **Anron Hool** — a skybarge operator
who passes through Adura's settled town during the Dorn years.
The Hool surname is intentional: a family lineage in skybarge work
running forward ~32,000 spins to Waj Hool, who owns and operates
The Porter in Book 1. Whether this echo is noted on the page is
the author's call.

**Third's encounter:** Third crosses Anka aboard this vessel during
the Dorn years — a job, a route, a contract. Unremarkable trip. He
notices the map because it's large and the material is unusual.
Notes it as *good craftsmanship, unusual material* and moves on.
He does not go still. He's not aboard long enough to see it change
— or the crossing is uneventful enough that no change occurs while
he watches. He disembarks. He doesn't mention it to Adura. She may
have a brief entry noting he crossed on a particular vessel:
practical, logistical, the way she records his movements. No
significance flagged.

**The loss:** after Dorn's death, Adura receives word of a job — a
skybarge crew seeking scouts and cartographers. She mentions it at
their next meeting outside of town, in the broker register she
always uses for contracts: practical, matter-of-fact, slightly
curious about the detail. She relays what she heard: the crew lost
their navigation map. Stolen. Irreplaceable. Half the size of the
ship — she notes this as the odd detail it is, because most just
use Humin-sized maps at the largest. Always current, they said:
showed new towns, washed-out roads, weather coming in. Sounds like
a tall tale, but they're offering good coin for replacements so
apparently it was real. She's already moving on to the contract
terms.

THE JURISDICTION GAP (ties to Legal & Dispute Infrastructure, Part
Nine): Adura, being practical, asks the obvious follow-up — who's
looking for whoever took it? The answer is a shrug. The vessel
belongs to no town; Anka's legal framework is built entirely on
locality, and a masterless skybarge, robbed somewhere over open
country between jurisdictions that don't overlap, falls into a
structural hole nobody's built to reach. Not neglect — there is
simply no one whose job it is. This is the practical reason the
crew hires replacements instead of pursuing a thief: replacing
what's gone is achievable; finding it belongs to no one. A single
dry line from Adura here suits her better than sentiment — she'd
find the gap more absurd than sad.

Third does the math. The material he noticed on that crossing. The
size. *Always current.* The specific things the crew can no longer
do, enumerated as replacements needed — a cartographer for new
settlements, a weather-watcher for incoming systems, scouts for
road conditions. Each replacement covers one thing the map did
simultaneously, automatically, without being asked.

He goes still. Adura notices between one sentence and the next — a
particular quality of stillness, different from the others she has
recorded. She keeps talking. She doesn't push. She writes it down
that evening without knowing what she wrote down.

He does not tell her what connected. He doesn't have a word for it
yet — only the shape of it: a Titan-willed object is gone from
where it was, and something took it deliberately, and this is not
the first time something has gone from where it was. He files it.
He keeps walking.

**What Third does not do:** he doesn't tell Adura. Doesn't take the
cartographer contract. Doesn't investigate — not ready to; doesn't
have enough yet to know what he'd be investigating *toward*. The
pattern has one data point. You cannot call one data point a
pattern. He walks.

## THE SOUGAD RUMOR

**What the rumor is:** a traveler's account, third-hand at minimum
by the time it reaches Third. Someone saw something, told someone,
told someone else. No story attached — nobody got close enough or
stayed long enough to make one. Just: a floating figure, passing
through, nobody approached, nobody saw where from or to.

What Sougad looks like to a bystander at this point: a figure
moving through the air under its own power — six magefire jars
grafted to the back providing lift, the way floatstone lifts a
skybarge, but on a person. Blue-white light from the jars. He
passed through a settlement near the eastern passage. Nobody
approached. Nobody saw where he went.

**Third's account to Adura (flat register):** *"A traveler from
the eastern passage reported seeing a floating figure near
[settlement]. Six vessels on the back. Magefire. Nobody approached
it. Nobody saw where it went."* No interpretation. No flag. He
moves on to the next item.

**Adura's journal entry (her translation):** she writes it down
verbatim — she writes everything down. Then adds one line: *"He
mentioned this the way he mentions weather. I wrote it down the
way I write everything. I am not sure why it seems worth noting,
except that he told it to me, which is not nothing."* She doesn't
know why she flagged it. She just did.

**What the reader does:** the Book 1 reader stops on *six
vessels.* Third carries two. Sougad carries six. That number is
the tell — the reader does the math Adura cannot do because she
doesn't know the math exists yet. Nobody in the book notices. The
entry sits there in the slat-book, between two other entries about
road conditions and a contract paid late, doing its work quietly.

**Placement:** Dorn years — active, travelling, rumors coming in
from the road. Not the settled middle, where Third's visits are
less frequent and the world feels smaller. Delivered as part of a
larger account Third gives Adura, not as a standalone report.
